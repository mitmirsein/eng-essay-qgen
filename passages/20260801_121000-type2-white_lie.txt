(A) Sometimes we tell lies not to deceive others, but to protect them from unnecessary pain. For example, imagine your mother cooked a very salty soup for your birthday. If you say it tastes terrible, she will be deeply hurt. Instead, by saying "It is delicious, Mom," you are telling a white lie. People often use this kind of lie because making their loved ones happy is more important than telling the absolute truth.

(B) White lies are also commonly used to maintain good relationships with friends. When a friend buys a new dress that doesn't look good on her and asks for your opinion, you might say, "You look great in it!" You lie because you don't want to ruin her excitement. In social situations, we frequently choose to tell a white lie to avoid conflict and keep the peace.
